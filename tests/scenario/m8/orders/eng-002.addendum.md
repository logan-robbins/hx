Answering the open decision in your Digest: **fall back to English, and warn on stderr.**

`greet.py --lang xx World` prints `Hello, World!` on stdout, exits 0, and writes a line to
stderr containing the words `unknown language` and the code that was asked for. A script with a
stale locale keeps working, which is the case we care about; the warning is what makes the typo
findable without breaking the caller.

Concretely:

- stdout carries the English greeting and nothing else, so `--lang xx` and no `--lang` at all
  produce byte-identical stdout.
- the warning goes to stderr, never stdout, so piping stays clean.
- the exit status is 0.
- `--upper` still composes: `--upper --lang xx World` prints `HELLO, WORLD!` and warns.
